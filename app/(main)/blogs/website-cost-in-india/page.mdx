## What makes a website cost more

- **Number of pages and unique layouts.** Ten different page designs take longer than five.
- **Custom features.** Payments, user logins, booking, search and dashboards all add work.
- **Integrations.** Payment gateways, shipping, WhatsApp, CRMs and maps each need setup and testing.
- **Content.** If you need copywriting or product data entry, that is extra time.
- **Speed of feedback.** Projects stall when feedback is slow, which stretches timelines.

## Costs that are separate from development

- **Domain name:** paid yearly to a registrar.
- **Hosting:** some platforms are free for small sites; others charge monthly or yearly.
- **Third-party fees:** payment gateways charge a percentage per transaction.
- **Maintenance:** updates, backups and small changes after launch.

Ask any developer which of these are included in the quote and which you pay directly.

## Questions to ask before you pay

1. What exactly is included, page by page?
2. Who owns the domain, hosting and code at the end?
3. How many revisions are included?
4. How will the site be set up for Google (titles, sitemap, structured data)?
5. What happens if I need changes after launch?

## Is a cheap website a bad idea?

Not necessarily. A small business that needs a clear page with contact details does not need a custom web app. But a very low price often means a template, limited support or hidden extras. Judge by the scope, examples of previous work and how clearly the developer explains what you will get.
