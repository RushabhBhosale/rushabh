# Portfolio direction study

## 1. The Field Notebook

1. **Central idea:** an evolving lab journal in which finished products, experiments, and lessons live on the same working pages.
2. **Why it fits:** Rushabh learns in public and builds across categories; the notebook makes curiosity, process, and writing inseparable.
3. **Hero:** a nearly blank sheet registers into place while the name is typeset, corrected, and annotated.
4. **Navigation:** thumb tabs and margin references rather than a conventional header.
5. **Projects:** large fold-out case-study pages that reveal the decision behind each product.
6. **Scroll:** page registration, layered sheets, and quiet pauses instead of a continuous effects reel.
7. **3D/WebGL:** optional shallow page depth only; the concept is strongest in the DOM.
8. **GSAP:** masks, page pinning, annotation paths, and fold-out timing.
9. **Typography:** editorial serif at display scale, utilitarian sans, compact monospaced notes.
10. **Visual language:** crop marks, paper layers, revision symbols, and one sharp signal color.
11. **Signature interaction:** hold a project to inspect the choices beneath the polished result.
12. **About/experience:** dated observations and working principles.
13. **Writing:** marginal references that expand into full article abstracts.
14. **Contact:** a final handwritten note with direct channels.
15. **Ending:** an unfinished page, ready for the next entry.

## 2. The Switchyard — selected

1. **Central idea:** a spatial routing world where web, mobile, desktop, tools, and writing are distinct lines connected by one instinct: shipping useful things.
2. **Why it fits:** Rushabh moves fluently between platforms without treating technologies as the story; the route is the method, and shipped work is the evidence.
3. **Hero:** the visitor enters a WebGL field of converging tracks while the name changes scale from distant marker to architectural type.
4. **Navigation:** a compact route control that can jump to key stations without breaking the journey.
5. **Projects:** each junction transforms the spatial track into a product interface with one decisive image, outcome, and platform signal.
6. **Scroll:** forward travel, junction pauses, a pinned inspection sequence, editorial decompression, and final convergence.
7. **3D/WebGL:** one major perspective field carries the entry and approach to selected work. It responds to pointer position and scroll velocity; the rest moves into accessible DOM layouts.
8. **GSAP:** camera-progress handoff, letter choreography, pinned project changes, masks, and convergence timing.
9. **Typography:** oversized condensed-feeling grotesk forms, quiet serif observations, and small mono coordinates.
10. **Visual language:** near-black space, ivory information, acid signal, cobalt branch, precise lines, and generous empty intervals.
11. **Signature interaction:** moving through a junction causes one track to occupy the entire frame and become the next project surface.
12. **About/experience:** a calm route history in which roles are stops and lessons are the connective line.
13. **Writing:** an editorial dispatch desk with titles doing the visual work.
14. **Contact:** all lines converge into one direct invitation.
15. **Ending:** a live signal continues beyond an empty future station marked NEXT.

## 3. The Shipping Floor

1. **Central idea:** a cinematic production line where rough ideas become tested, documented, and shipped artifacts.
2. **Why it fits:** it foregrounds Rushabh's bias toward finishing rather than merely knowing tools.
3. **Hero:** a large working/shipping drum rotates into alignment.
4. **Navigation:** an operator rail with stages instead of pages.
5. **Projects:** inspection plates stamped with outcome, role, and release state.
6. **Scroll:** vertical assembly, horizontal inspection, then a return to vertical dispatch.
7. **3D/WebGL:** one tactile release drum, rendered with material depth.
8. **GSAP:** stamps, rollers, masks, and weighted mechanical easing.
9. **Typography:** industrial grotesk paired with human notes.
10. **Visual language:** dark steel, paper labels, vermilion marks, and hard working light.
11. **Signature interaction:** dragging the drum advances to another shipped artifact.
12. **About/experience:** the operator log.
13. **Writing:** the documentation desk.
14. **Contact:** the outgoing dock.
15. **Ending:** one empty production slot waiting for the next build.

## Selected storyboard: The Switchyard

### 00 — Signal / entry (0–100vh)

The screen starts almost silent: a point of acid light, a small coordinate, and darkness. Perspective tracks resolve around it. “RUSHABH BHOSALE” arrives in two different depth planes: the first name is close and cropped, the surname is distant and then catches up. Pointer movement subtly shifts the WebGL camera. Scrolling begins forward motion.

### 01 — Enter the system (100–210vh)

The route field accelerates, then branches. The verbs BUILD / EXPERIMENT / SHIP / LEARN / WRITE cross the screen as physical markers, one at a time, rather than as a skills list. A short statement stays anchored while the scale of the type changes around it. Near the end, the strongest track widens until it becomes the frame edge of the project interface.

### 02 — Selected work junctions (210–610vh)

The viewport pins. Each quarter of the scroll changes the active junction. The WebGL route remains behind the interface, but becomes quieter. A project image enters as a screen mounted in space, then settles into a crisp two-dimensional inspection view. Title, number, platform, outcome, and links move on separate timings. The sequence covers StackPort, ClipNote, AnimeSparks, Headline Thread, and the Expense Tracker. Desktop buttons can also select a station; mobile receives the same content as a direct vertical sequence.

### 03 — Switchboard / experiments (610–740vh)

The interface breaks apart into route fragments. Smaller tools and experiments occupy a deliberately sparse switchboard. Hovering or focusing a line energizes only that route; there are no generic cards or technology logos.

### 04 — Dispatches / writing (740–860vh)

Motion falls away. The background turns ivory and the route becomes a fine reading rule. Article titles lead at editorial scale, with dates and short descriptions set quietly. The shift from spectacle to stillness makes writing feel intentional, not secondary.

### 05 — Route history / experience (860–980vh)

The palette returns to dark, but the camera is now still. A single vertical line records roles as stops. Each stop emphasizes what was built or learned rather than a technology inventory. This section has the longest pauses and least motion.

### 06 — Convergence / contact (980–1100vh)

Routes from every side bend inward. Contact copy grows from a coordinate label into the final large line: “BRING ME THE NEXT HARD THING.” Email, GitHub, LinkedIn, and résumé remain direct and visible. All lines meet at one signal, then continue past an empty station marked NEXT / UNASSIGNED.

## Interaction and accessibility rules

- WebGL is enhancement, not the content layer; all project and contact information remains semantic DOM content.
- The canvas pauses outside the spatial chapters and reduces its pixel density on smaller devices.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` removes pinning, camera travel, animated type, and loader delay.
- Keyboard focus receives the same route emphasis as hover.
- Body copy is at least 16px, controls are at least 44px, contrast remains strong, and project imagery has useful alt text.
- Mobile changes the choreography rather than shrinking it: stacked stations, shorter headings, no scroll-jacking, and touch-safe actions.
